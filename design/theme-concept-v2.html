Slab/Kit

The interface is a piece of field hardware you could take apart. Panels are machined slabs that swing open on hinges. Tools are expansion modules that click into a rail. Screws, etched part numbers and exploded views show how it's put together. The wavelength accent from Spectral Plate carries over: every LED, readout and lit key glows in the colour of the band you're viewing.

Six ideas

01

Slabs on hinges

Panels are machined slabs that swing open in sequence on a right-hand hinge, with a short mechanical overshoot. They close the same way.

02

Tools are modules

Analysis tools are expansion modules that slot into a rail. You lift one out and click another in. Seated modules light up.

03

Show the build

Screws, etched part numbers and seams are visible on purpose. Hold Alt and any panel opens into an exploded view with labelled parts.

04

One light source

Everything is matte graphite. The only colour comes from LEDs, readouts and the primary key, all lit in the current band.

05

Keys travel

Buttons are physical keys with 3 px of travel and a hard bottom, so every press feels like a real control.

06

Pixel seams

Corners keep Spectral Plate's 2 px stair-steps, so the machined parts and the pixel-art Voyager share one grid.

Module anatomy · hover to explode

Every module has the same four parts, and the interface shows them. In the app this is a real interaction: hold Alt over a panel to see it come apart into labelled layers. It's mostly for delight, and it also teaches what each part of the tool does.

The exploded view uses the same hinge easing, so the whole kit moves like one machine.

Voyager's voice · dial it

Personality · settings

These are real settings in the app. They go to the model as two numbers, which keeps the system prompt small.
